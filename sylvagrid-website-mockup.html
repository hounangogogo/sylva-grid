Why it's hard

Sourcing cells from China isn't the bottleneck.
Trusting what arrives is.

Pack manufacturers outside China don't struggle to find a factory — they struggle to find one they can verify, communicate with in real time, and hold accountable after the invoice is paid.

Language & Distance

Specs get lost in translation

Datasheets, tolerances, and quality flags don't always survive the trip from a Chinese factory floor to a foreign engineering team — and by the time the gap surfaces, the cells are already on a boat.

Compliance

Missing paperwork stops shipments cold

UN 38.3 test summaries, MSDS, and CO get requested after the fact more often than they should. Without them, cargo sits at customs — or worse, doesn't clear at all.

Continuity

One factory relationship is a single point of failure

A single-supplier line stalls the moment that factory has a bad quarter, a capacity shift, or a policy change. Pack production shouldn't depend on one phone number in Shenzhen.

How we work

Compliance, by default

We don't sell you the cell.
We sell you the paper trail behind it.

Transport Canada's own testing found that half of sampled third-party replacement lithium batteries failed UN 38.3 — some catastrophically. We built our process around that exact failure point.

UN 38.3 test summaries, verified against batch

We confirm the test summary corresponds to the exact production run being shipped — not a general certification for the product line.

Full documentation package, before departure

MSDS, certificate of origin, and transport declarations are assembled and checked prior to the cargo leaving the factory.

A record you can hand to your own QA team

Every shipment comes with a documentation file your compliance or engineering team can audit independently.
